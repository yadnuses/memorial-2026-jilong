CHAPTER 03 · RESCUE AND NAMES

The debris flow stopped.
The disaster did not.
In the 72 hours after 26 August, rescue began on both sides of the border line at once.
And with every day that followed, a list grew longer.
SCROLL
MOBILIZATION · 72 HOURS

Rescue began on both banks at almost the same moment

Once the flow had crossed the border, Gyirong Port on the Chinese side and Rasuwagadhi on the Nepali side were separated by nothing but a broken bridge. The two rescue efforts unfolded at once, each from its own bank — one operation divided by a border, both aimed at the same valley.

Xinhua · China News Service · NDRRMA bulletins
CHINESE SIDE

Clear the site first, then move in

Before the flood reached the port, officers of the Gyirong Entry-Exit Border Inspection Station raced to evacuate everyone on site. In their ranks was a newly sworn officer born in the 2000s (Wu Yanlu).

Huang Peng, deputy commander of the second duty team (born 1991), stayed behind in the last moments before the flow reached the port, directing the evacuation and turning back to call people out of the visitor service point. The Tibet Border Inspection General Station raised its response to Level I and sent 346 officers from Lhasa and Shigatse to reinforce Gyirong Township, with life detectors, breaching tools and other equipment for search, rescue and evacuation.

China News Service · National Immigration Administration briefing (late August 2026)
NEPALI SIDE

Helicopters and a registration desk

Nepali Army helicopters flew repeated evacuation runs around Rasuwagadhi, while NDRRMA activated its rescued persons registry within hours of the disaster. Name, age and sex were entered record by record, becoming the only public record families could search. Beyond basic identity, each entry also noted where the person was rescued and their condition, to help with medical triage and later headcounts.

NDRRMA registry (bipad.gov.np) · The Kathmandu Post
SPECIALIST TEAM

Nine people, heading for the tunnel

China sent nine tunnel-rescue specialists across the border into Nepal. Their destination was the Upper Trishuli 3A hydropower tunnel, built by a Chinese contractor: the flow had poured inside, sealing the portal with mud and boulders. Hundreds of workers were trapped in the tunnel and the underground powerhouse when the disaster struck, some cut off deep inside.
